# Phase 3 Analyst Brief — Sable Offshore (SOC US)

Date: 2026-04-08T20:45:30+00:00 | Verdict: Stop

## Thesis Summary
Sable Offshore operates offshore oil assets in federal waters off California, primarily the Santa Ynez Unit with three platforms that have been shut-in since 2015. The company faces a binary turnaround scenario requiring either pipeline regulatory clearance or implementation of an offshore storage strategy, both demanding significant capital while burning through cash reserves. With only $97.7 million in cash against $921.6 million in debt maturing March 2027, the investment presents unacceptable execution risk relative to potential returns. [1] [1]

## Key Findings
- **Liquidity Crisis with Imminent Debt Maturity**: The company faces severe financial distress with a current ratio effectively below 0.15x, holding just $97.7 million in cash against $921.6 million in current debt obligations. The Senior Secured Term Loan matures in March 2027, creating a forced refinancing scenario complicated by the company's inability to generate operating cash flows while pipeline restrictions remain in place. [1] [1]
- **Operational Restart Driving Massive Cash Burn**: Operating expenses surged 130% to $219.2 million in 2025 as the company pursued production restart efforts, while generating zero revenue due to pipeline transportation restrictions. The company burned $351.7 million in operating cash flow during 2025, indicating an unsustainable burn rate that will exhaust cash reserves well before debt maturity without immediate revenue generation. [1] [1]
- **Binary Strategic Choice with Vastly Different Capital Requirements**: Management faces two drastically different strategic paths forward: pursuing pipeline regulatory approvals requiring $100-200 million in additional capital expenditures, or implementing an offshore storage and transfer strategy demanding approximately $475 million. This 2.4x differential suggests management lacks a clear execution roadmap and may be forced into the more expensive option if regulatory delays persist. [1] [1]
- **Revenue Generation Blocked by Regulatory Uncertainty**: Despite restarting production in May 2025, the company cannot transport petroleum through Pipeline Segments 324 and 325 due to regulatory restrictions stemming from a 2015 crude oil release incident. Revenue recognition remains completely dependent on securing regulatory clearances that have proven elusive for over a decade, creating binary execution risk with no clear timeline for resolution. [1] [1]

## Top Risks
- **Going Concern Qualification Creates Forced Seller Dynamic**: Auditors have issued a going concern qualification due to uncertainty over regulatory approvals and financing availability. This creates a ticking clock scenario where the company must secure both pipeline approval and additional capital before March 2027, potentially forcing management into disadvantageous financing or asset sale transactions to avoid insolvency. [1]
- **Regulatory Delays Could Trigger Liquidity Crisis**: The company's survival depends entirely on securing regulatory clearances to resume petroleum transportation, with no alternative revenue streams available. Any material delays in regulatory approvals could exhaust the company's cash reserves before debt maturity, particularly given the current $351.7 million annual cash burn rate during restart operations. [1]
- **Binary Capital Choice Amplifies Execution Risk**: The massive differential between the two strategic options ($100-200M vs $475M) suggests management may be forced into the more expensive offshore storage strategy if pipeline approvals remain elusive. This would require raising additional capital equivalent to roughly 5x current cash levels while already facing severe liquidity constraints and going concern issues. [1]
